## 🏙️ Urban Governance in India: Institutional Evolution, Constitutional Design & Post-1992 Reform 

**_Independent Research Project · Governance, Planning & Public Institutions_**

This project is a comprehensive, book-length analysis tracing how India’s urban governance system evolved from **Mughal land institutions** and **colonial municipalization** to the constitutional and statutory frameworks that define modern cities. It examines how institutions—🛡️ cantonments, 🏛️ municipal bodies, 🗂️ development authorities, and 🏢 planning commissions—were historically designed for centralized control and how these legacies continue to shape contemporary urban outcomes. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}  

The study synthesizes **150+ years of laws, reforms, and planning regimes**, culminating in a detailed evaluation of the **73rd & 74th Constitutional Amendments** and the persistent gaps in fiscal, administrative, and planning devolution across states. It integrates analysis of India’s shift from the Planning Commission’s centralized Five-Year Plans to modern missions—including **JNNURM, AMRUT, and Smart Cities**—to reveal how federal–state dynamics influence service delivery, urban finance, and institutional accountability. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  

Overall, the work positions Indian urban governance as a **layered, negotiated, and evolving institutional architecture**, offering a structured reference for practitioners and policymakers working at the intersection of 🏙️ urban planning, ⚖️ constitutional design, and 🧭 governance reform.
